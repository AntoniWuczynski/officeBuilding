# DESIGN.md — Office Building
Date: 2026-09-29   Status: diverged
Codified from: new (the previous scaffold's dark oklch theme was an agent default, never an owner decision, and is replaced)
Identity: The only agent manager where your coding agents work in a cutaway office building you can see into, and every manager's panel is an object on the floor.
Volume default: canvas-first. The building and floor scenes are the content and carry the identity. Chrome around them (directory bar, rail, sidebar, terminal) is quiet signage: paper plates, ink borders, no shadows.

  Props (objects in the room, never chrome): --roof #8E887C, --window #AFD3E6, --monitor #2B2E33 / #40444C / #1F2226, --cork #C99A5B / #8C6436, --brass #C9A45C / #8A6D2E, desk plant (agent in a git worktree, A Human 2026-10-06) leaves #6F9A4E / #557A3A, soil #4A3626, pot #B8683F / #96512F, sticky notes #F7E27A / #9FD8F2 / #F6B5C8, manila folder (a finished subagent's work, A Human 2026-10-11) #E3C27A / #B08A3E, --terminal #16181C with #E8E6E1 text

Since 2026-11-22 (GitHub issue #14, A Human 2026-11-19) a worker whose model is in a tier is one of four more characters, kit, nell, otto and vera, each drawn in six builds, and the six older workers are kept for every other model. See Look below.
  Desk woods (A Human 2026-11-20, scene colours kept in src/lib/woods.ts, never chrome), as top / front / side: pale maple #F1C080 / #BC9664 / #95774F (Claude Code), wenge #1B0E0A / #150B08 / #110906 (Codex), oak #B26E2A / #8B5621 / #6E441A (Cursor), walnut #4D2B1A / #3C2214 / #301B10 (opencode), cherry #9F4115 / #7C3310 / #63280D (Antigravity), pine #F2BA62 / #BD914C / #96733D (an unknown tool). Each top is the mean colour of its generated grain swatch (art/wood/raw), and the front and side are that colour darkened. The flat colour is always painted, with the grain tile over it on the top face only. --timber stays for the manager's desk and the other furniture.
  --ultracode: #8F5BDB (the tint on the worker of a Claude Code desk hired with ultracode on, and its chip in the legend, nothing else)
  Pets (A Human 2026-10-09): what an agent leaves running stands in front of its desk, cut out the same way. A shell is a seashell, Claude Code's Monitor tool an owl, a browser it drives a cat, any other program a dog.
Type:
  Signage: "DIN Condensed" (macOS system font, bold), uppercase allowed only on physical signs (roof sign, floor nameplates). Fallback "Arial Narrow", sans-serif.
  Numerals: "DIN Alternate" bold for counts and floor numbers (tabular). Fallback the mono stack.
  Interface text: the macOS system face (-apple-system). Recorded decision: it is a native desktop app and the chrome speaks the platform's voice.
  Code, paths, terminal: "SF Mono", Menlo, monospace.
Motion:
  Characters: South Park-style cut-out animation (A Human 2026-10-09): flat pieces hinged at shoulder, elbow, neck and hips that snap between keyframes (steps), quick and exaggerated, never smooth. The loops encode session state: typing in bursts with forearms striking the keyboard, hand on chin when thinking, arm up and waving when it needs you, sitting back and looking round when idle, slumped when finished. Upper arms tuck behind the torso and forearms sit in front of it. A stuck agent (error) lies flat on the floor in front of the desk, eyes shut, with a blinking red "!" (owner request, 2026-09-29). Semantic, not decorative.
  Pets pop in with an overshoot when their program starts and pop out when it ends.
  Navigation: one camera move per action (dive into a floor, pull back to the building), 450-600ms tween, ease-out, no bounce, no overshoot.
  Agent-to-agent messages: a worker walks a note to the recipient's desk. Messages are also read from the transcripts of sessions that sent each other messages.
  prefers-reduced-motion: character loops stop, camera moves become cuts, walkers teleport.
Signature device: the metaphor made structural. Every sidebar section is an object in the room and opens its panel: memo pile on the manager's desk = Decisions queue, cork board = Human TODO, whiteboard = AI TODO, meter on the wall = Spend, brass plaque = Info, clipboard sign-out sheet by the door = Signed out (sessions to resume, A Human 2026-10-01), "Your terminal" over the manager's office = Shells (the human's own plain shells, Cmd+N, A Human 2026-10-11), an agent walking a note to a colleague = agent-to-agent message, a finishing subagent walking a manila folder to its agent = its work handed in (A Human 2026-10-11), the manager at the desk in the manager's office, or the "Hire a manager" tag on that desk while nobody is hired = Manager (the floor's manager agent, its waiting notes and its log, a call the build made on 2026-11-12), fuse box beside the toolbox in the plant room = Settings (app-wide, also Cmd+,, A Human 2026-11-12). The fuse box is drawn in the plant room's grey metal (the --duct tokens), with no new colour. Settings is the one panel that is a dialog plate and not a sidebar section, because it also opens from the building view, which has no sidebar. It never shares the screen with another plate or the docked terminal: opening one closes the other.
Menus: a right-click on a worker, and a click or right-click on a helper working in a git worktree, opens a small "Open in…" menu that lists the installed editors (a call the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). It is a paper plate with an ink border and no shadow, drawn flat above the scene like the worker's hover card, so the walls cannot slice it. It closes on a press outside it, on Escape, when the window loses focus and when the floor is zoomed. It is a menu and not a panel, so the hard no on a panel overlapping a panel does not cover it. The "Open in" button with its arrow is chrome on a panel's header, the sign-out sheet and the Info panel, and adds no object to the room. Since 2026-11-20 a worker's menu may end with one more line that is not an editor, "Take over with a new session", for a Claude Code or Codex session (a call the build made, not yet seen by A Human).
Shared boards: a checklist file named in Settings under Boards is read like a floor's Human TODO file, and the second pipe column of a line is its project tag (no new object in the room). A last reply is a session's latest reply to A Human, cut to its first sentence of at most 140 characters.
Board items: every item on the cork board and the whiteboard that comes from somewhere carries a small text badge saying where (calls the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). A file item shows its file's name, an item from Linear shows "Linear · ENG-123", and an item from the app's own records shows nothing. The badge is mono type on paper with an ink border, with no logo and no emoji. A Linear badge is a button that opens the issue in the browser. An action that changes something outside the app asks first in a row inside the list ("Mark ENG-123 as Done in Linear?"), never in a dialog, because a panel must not overlap a panel. Each board that shows Linear items has one status line with a Re-check button. The link between a floor and Linear is a row on the brass plaque's Info panel, and the key is a group in Settings. Connectors add no object to the room.
QA desk: a floor whose quality assurance (QA) is switched on has one more desk, in its front-left corner, with a magnifying glass on it (calls the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). The glass is drawn in CSS from tokens already listed, an --ink ring with a --window fill and a --timber-front handle, with no image and no new colour. The reviewer sits at this desk while it works and is drawn like every other worker. The room grows a little deeper, unless it is at its minimum depth, so the last row of desks keeps clear of the corner. The desk's tag reads "QA" and opens the brass plaque's Info panel, where the "Quality assurance" row holds the switch, the status, the last reviews and Check now. The result is a drawn rubber stamp on that tag and on the floor's nameplate in the building view. It reads "QA passed" in --ok or "QA notes" in --wait, in signage type, uppercase because a stamp is a physical mark, with a border in its own colour, tilted 6 degrees, with no fill and no shadow. The words differ as well as the colour, so it does not rely on colour alone. It is never an emoji. Switching QA on leads to posts on GitHub, so it asks first in a row inside the Info panel, never in a dialog. The settings are a group in Settings.
Manager: the cut-out in the manager's office is the floor's manager, an agent that hands work to the desks (calls the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). It adds one object to the room, the manager at its desk, which opens a Manager section in the sidebar. With nobody hired the chair is empty and the desk carries a small paper tag reading "Hire a manager", drawn like the tags on the other room objects. On the Setup floor the chair is empty and there is no tag. Once hired, the seventh cast character sits there in the pose of its session's state and is a button. The lamp on its desk uses the status tokens and no new colour: --ok while it works, --wait while a call it escalated is open, --off while it is paused. The manager walks its own notes out from its office, as workers do, and its chair is empty while it walks. A desk the manager drives carries the word "managed" under its name tag, in mono type on paper with an ink border, like a board item's badge. A desk with notes waiting carries a sticky note in the existing sticky-note yellow with an ink border, tilted 4 degrees, showing how many wait. The worker's label and hover card also say in words that the desk is managed and why a note waits. The Manager panel is a sidebar section and not a dialog. Its switches (Paused, "May hire desks") act at once, and it has no confirmation row. Text that comes from the manager is shown as plain text only, never as Markdown or links. The panel has no button that ends the manager, and it says to type /exit in the manager's terminal. The settings are a group in Settings. Added on 2026-11-20 (calls the build made, not yet seen by A Human): a desk with a note waiting also shows it as a strip inside its docked panel, drawn like the route strip with the 8px lamp in --wait, holding the note's first 140 characters, the reason it waits in words and a Withdraw button drawn like the dark "Open in" button. The manager may now end a desk on its floor (HUMAN_DECISIONS OB-333), and the panel's hint says so and that pausing stops it. An ending adds no button and no object. It is a line in the panel's log and a second line on the sign-out sheet, "Ended by the floor's manager", with the reason.
Chat: a desk's docked panel has two faces, terminal and chat, in the same dark palette, --terminal with its text colour (calls the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). It is still the one docked panel, so the chat adds no object to the room and no new panel type. A chat opens at 60% of the floor's height and stays resizable, and it remembers its own height apart from the terminal's. A desk that keeps a terminal has a two-button switch in the panel's head, Chat and Terminal, drawn like the "Open in" button, and the pressed one is inverted. An agent's message is Markdown with raw HTML off. A link or an image is never an element. It is drawn as text with its address, so nothing in a chat can be clicked or loaded. A tool call is a card that folds, and its command, output and diffs are plain mono text. A diff uses the status tokens for its lines, --ok for added and --fault for removed, with a + or - in front, so it does not rely on colour alone. Thinking and long text are folded, long text behind a "Show all" button. A permission prompt on a terminal desk is a card with one button, "Answer in the terminal", and the chat never answers for A Human. A waiting message sits above the message box with the reason it waits and a Withdraw button. No new tokens, no emoji and no internal names in the interface.
Routing: provider routing adds no object to the room (calls the build made on 2026-11-12, not yet seen by A Human). It is a group in Settings and an "Account context" row on the brass plaque's Info panel. Inside the group each account context and each connection is a card, paper with a 1px ink border and no shadow, and a connection's card folds. A card is part of the Settings plate and not a panel of its own. Removing a context or a connection asks in a row inside its card, never in a dialog. A connection's kind is shown by its name ("Claude subscription", "Anthropic API key"), never by its internal name. A routed desk shows its route as a strip inside its docked panel, between the panel's head and the terminal or chat, in the panel's dark palette (a call the build made on 2026-11-13, not yet seen by A Human). The strip is part of the one docked panel and not a panel of its own. It pushes the terminal down and covers nothing. It carries an 8px square lamp in the status tokens, --ok while a connection serves the desk, --wait before the first request and while the active connection has a problem, and --fault while the desk is blocked, and the state is always written beside it in words ("Route: Blocked"), so it does not rely on colour alone. A Details button unfolds the desk's order inside the panel, up to 45% of its height, with a Retry and a "Switch to this" button drawn like the dark "Open in" button. A blocked route is a plain sentence and never a spinner. No new tokens.
Handover: handing a desk over adds no object to the room and no panel (calls the build made on 2026-11-13, not yet seen by A Human). A used-up desk shows a strip inside its docked panel, above the route strip and drawn like it, with the same 8px lamp in the status tokens, --wait while the desk waits for A Human or is being handed over, --fault when it cannot be handed over or the handover failed and --ok once it has been, and the state is always written beside it in words. An offered desk has a button that names the receiving tool, "Hand over to Codex" or "Hand over to Claude Code", drawn like the dark "Open in" button. It asks in a row inside the panel that names what is ended, never in a dialog. A worker carries one word under its name tag, "used up", "handing over" or where it came from ("from Claude Code", "from Codex"), drawn like "managed". The sign-out sheet says in a second line where a session was handed over. In Settings it is one more group, with the switch, one choice for each direction that preselects nothing and three hints. Every sentence takes its tool names as data, so the second direction added no object, strip or string set of its own. No new tokens. A take-over (HUMAN_DECISIONS OB-334, calls the build made on 2026-11-20, not yet seen by A Human) adds no object and no panel either. A desk's docked panel has one more strip of the same kind, holding a "Take over with a new session" button drawn like the dark "Open in" button and, once a take-over has happened, the lamp in --ok and a sentence saying who took over from whom. The button opens the Hire sheet in a take-over mode, the same dialog plate with the same pickers, where nothing is chosen until A Human chooses and the warning that the old session keeps running is a sentence inside the sheet.
Requests: an agent's open question or approval adds no object to the room and no panel (calls the build made on 2026-11-20 for GitHub issue #12, not yet seen by A Human). It is shown in the two places that exist, its row in the queue (the memo pile's Decisions, the top bar's Needs you, the place panel's rows) and its card in the desk's conversation. A chat desk has one card for each open request. A row for a chat desk opens the conversation at that card, which carries a 2px outline in --wait while it is the one asked for, and the keyboard goes to its first field or to the card, never to a choice button. A card whose answer is on its way takes no second click, a card whose answer was not sent says "Not sent" with the reason above its choices, and a card for a question that does not stop the agent says so in a sentence. A row answered in a terminal never shows an answer form. It carries one sentence that says where the agent runs and a button only for what the app can do, "Open its terminal", "Bring its Terminal tab to the front" or "Bring Ghostty to the front" with the app's own name, and no button where there is nothing to bring forward. Opening the terminal never closes the row. A worker's hover card gains two plain lines, "Has a question for you" for a working desk with a question open, and a sentence saying that the building cannot see approval prompts there and why. Text from a request is shown as plain text only. No new tokens. Codex's sub-agents add no object and no panel either (calls the build made on 2026-11-21 for GitHub issue #12, not yet seen by A Human). They sit at the tiny desks sub-agents already have, drawn the same way. A request a sub-agent raised says who asked in words, ", by its helper Ada" on its row and "Asked by its helper Ada." on its card, and that helper's tiny desk shows the --wait light. A row for a chat desk's question in plain prose says "Reply in its conversation." with the button "Open its conversation", and shows no answer form.
Look: a desk says what runs there (GitHub issue #14, A Human 2026-11-19 and 2026-11-20. How each rule is carried out is a call the build made on 2026-11-22, not yet seen by A Human). It adds no object to the room and no panel. The desk's wood is the tool. Claude Code and Codex are the lightest and the darkest of the six woods, so the two main tools differ in lightness as well as hue. The worker is the model. A model whose id holds a family word as a whole word is drawn as that tier's worker: kit for Haiku and Luna, nell for Sonnet and Terra, otto for Opus and Sol, vera for Fable and Astra. Any other model, and a missing one, keeps the worker its session's id picks from the six older ones, so nobody is ever blank. The build is the effort, lean to buff, as minimal, low, medium, high, extra high and max. Codex's ultra is drawn as max, and an effort that is not reported, or is outside the table, as medium. The builds are six drawn sheets and not one figure scaled. Every joint is placed from the medium build's sizes, so shoulders, elbows, neck and hips sit at the same coordinates in all six, and the head, the closed-eyes head and the chair are the medium sheet's in every build. The manager keeps its one look and the plain timber desk. A helper at a tiny desk is drawn by its own model and effort, each falling back to its agent's, on its agent's wood, and is never tinted. The tint is one colour filter that mixes 18% of --ultracode into every piece and leaves the outline alone. It marks a Claude Code desk that was hired from the Hire sheet with ultracode on, and its words say exactly that, "Hired with ultracode on". Every view draws a worker through one component, so the floor, the cutaway, the Islands overview and an island's inside agree. On the Islands overview a hut's counter takes the wood's flat colour, and inside an island the crate does. The picture is never the only signal. The hover card and the worker's accessible name say the tool, the model and the effort, the hover card says when a desk was hired with ultracode on and what its helpers run on, and the hover card, the place panel's rows and the sign-out sheet show the worker's face beside the words. The legend is a folded list at the end of the brass plaque's Info panel, "What the looks mean", in paper and ink with no shadow. It is drawn from the same tables as the room, with words beside every picture. No emoji and no internal names.
First-paint emotion: command-play (a management sim: you are in charge, and it is a toy you want to poke). Owner picked "Command and Play"; recorded as one compound.
Voice: matter-of-fact British English. States in human words: Working, Thinking, Needs you, Stuck, Idle, Finished. Errors say what happened and what to do next. No em dashes in interface copy.
Dark mode: deferred (daylight only)
Hard-no exceptions: none
Rejected directions: not chosen on 2026-09-29 (drafts page): B night tower, C architect's axonometric, signature 1 lift ride, signature 3 16-colour pixel constraint. Owner may revisit; agents do not re-propose them.
Implementation constraints:
  CSS 3D rig. Building: rotateX(68deg) rotateZ(18deg), shallow storeys (depth 80, height 56) with desks near the front so the slab above never hides a worker. Single floor: rotateX(60deg) rotateZ(45deg), no ceiling.
  Billboards (people, bubbles, nameplates) counter-rotate with rotateZ(-z) rotateX(-x) so they always face the camera.
  Tauri renders in WKWebView: every scene change must be checked in WebKit, not only Chromium.
  Minimum window 900x600 (tauri.conf.json). That is the small-viewport floor. The 320px web floor does not apply to this desktop app.
Overviews (added 2026-11-13, proposed in a pull request): the building stays the default; Islands is a second drawing of the same floors, chosen by a switch in the top bar and remembered. Only the drawing changes: the top bar, usage signs, dialogs, floor screen and rail are the same components, so every ability exists in both. Islands uses the same chrome (paper plates, ink borders, sign type), one sea behind everything with the list and usage signs floating on it, and the building's motion (islands rise on open, lift under the pointer, sink when removed; a move to an island is one 520 ms ease-out; reduced motion skips all of it). Search dims non-matching islands with their huts, people and labels. Each agent is a person in a hut, a worktree agent has a palm where the office has a desk plant, and islands carry no huts of their own. Wording follows the overview (floor or island, demolish or sink). Island props (src/archipelago/build.ts, scene colours, never chrome): sky and fog #8FD3F4, sea #1F9BE0, sand #F0D9A0, grass #6CC04A / #4FA03A, rock #8A8F99 / #6B7079, hut and dock wood #B9824A, trunk and hut wall #8A5A33, palm leaves #2F9E44, hut roofs #D9534F, lighthouse #F4F1EA, dishes #DFE6EE.
Place panel (proposed in a pull request, not yet seen by A Human): a floor's nameplate in Office, or an island in Islands, opens one paper plate with an ink border at the right of the view. It holds the place's agents, each with a dot when it has news since A Human last opened it (the top bar counts them), its last reply and any question it waits on, plus rename, Move to, tags, Enter, Sink or Demolish, and Throw out or Exile on every row but the manager's and the QA reviewer's. Two top-bar buttons drop plates of the same kind beside it: "Needs you" (every open question on the map, and the named sessions with no project) and "Boards" (the shared checklist files). They keep the hard no on a panel overlapping a panel: only one of the three is open at a time, opening one closes the others, and opening Settings closes the two top-bar plates. They add no object to the room.
Island inside (from pull request #13, whose author reports A Human saying "i like the open air" on 2026-11-16, not yet confirmed by A Human here): an island's floor is open air, so the wall objects of "every sidebar section is an object in the room" (Human TODO, TODO, Spend, Info) are reached from the rail there. Each agent sits behind a crate in the overview's hut (dark back wall, two posts, red roof whose front edge clears the name tag), the manager in a thatched hut in a light blue shirt, with no monitors or office chairs. Four palms stand off the room's corners on a disc of sand in a lagoon, drawn on the floor so they zoom and pan with it. In both views the manager's two tags hang clear of the tallest tag at the desk beside them.
Hard nos (project-local additions): emoji as icons; internal enum names in the interface (waiting-human, raise-window); a panel overlapping another panel; search that removes storeys from the building (non-matching floors dim instead).
